Operation-aware Multimodal Clinical Decision Support System
for ICU Discharge Risk Assessment
Guoqing Zhang,1 Rafik Hadfi,1 Takayuki Ito1
1Graduate School of Informatics, Kyoto University, Kyoto, Japan
zhang.guoqing.27t@st.kyoto-u.ac.jp, rafik.hadfi@i.kyoto-u.ac.jp, ito@i.kyoto-u.ac.jp
Abstract
Unplanned 30-day intensive care unit (ICU) readmission following hospital discharge is a major benchmark for evaluating healthcare quality and patient safety in modern critical
care medicine. Most existing predictive approaches assume
that readmission risk is determined solely by a patient’s individual pathophysiological state, ignoring the institution’s
resource capacity and operational context (Hospital Operational Context). In clinical practice, discharge decisions are
frequently dynamic trade-offs made by intensivists balancing patient medical stability against ward bed turnover pressures. To address this gap, we design an Operation-aware
Multimodal Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) for
ICU discharge risk assessment. On the one hand, the system unites multi-agent collaborative extraction with selfevolving retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to eliminate
formatting-template noise across lengthy progress notes. On
the other hand, it constructs departmental workload indicators (icu_speedup_los) alongside admission flow characteristics, unifying continuous vital signs, hierarchical sparse
medical codes (ICD/DRG), and discharge summary semantics. Addressing the extreme class imbalance (∼10.5% readmission incidence) and the challenge of alarm fatigue, the
system implements data normalization, class reweighting optimization, and an F1 decision-threshold calibration pipeline.
In 20 independent multi-seed runs conducted on a cohort of
20,000 ICU stays from MIMIC-III, empirical findings show
that: a single-modality baseline depending exclusively on vital
signs possesses limited predictive capacity under real-world
class imbalance (PR-AUC of only 0.1387 ± 0.0086); directly concatenating raw text embeddings causes performance
degradation in decision tree models (XGBoost and LightGBM PR-AUC drop to 0.3893 and 0.4030, respectively);
whereas an end-to-end multimodal fine-tuning architecture
(Transformer E2E FineTune) utilizing layer-wise parameter
freezing and differential learning rates successfully reverses
text negative transfer, achieving the overall highest AUROC
of 0.8288±0.0110, PR-AUC of 0.4373±0.0310, and a competitive F1-Score of 0.4757±0.0196. Mechanistic attribution
analyses also confirm that operational indicators supply independent risk signals, establishing an engineering foundation
for deploying high-utility clinical decision support tools in
bedside hospital wards.

S1
Introduction
In intensive care clinical decision-making, identifying vulnerable patients at risk of latent deterioration and avoiding
premature step-down to general wards are core imperatives of
critical care medicine. Patients experiencing unplanned ICU
readmission within 30 days of discharge undergo substantially prolonged hospital stays and face elevated in-hospital
mortality (Klinge et al. 2020; Barbieri et al. 2020; Sheetrit,
Brief, and Elisha 2023). While recent multicenter investigations confirm the promise of machine learning for postdischarge surveillance (Lim et al. 2025), existing clinical
early warning systems commonly encounter barriers during
ward deployment due to alarm fatigue: in low-prevalence
locales where readmissions occur in only ∼10% of cases,
typical models optimized for high AUROC tend to trigger
substantial false-positive alarms, prompting clinicians to turn
off the alert systems. To construct an actionable Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS), one must preserve screening
sensitivity while substantially improving precision, thereby
driving PR-AUC and F1 scores toward clinically acceptable
operating points.
Although electronic health record (EHR) repositories such
as MIMIC-III (Johnson et al. 2016) have advanced machine learning in critical care—spanning deep temporal focus (Madinei, Keyvanpour, and Shojaedini 2024), graphical
EHR representations (Choi et al. 2020), and recent readmission benchmarks (Fathy, Emeriaud, and Cheriet 2025)—
state-of-the-art predictive models still encounter two fundamental deployment bottlenecks. First, they exhibit a neglect
of hospital operational context. Standard models conceptualize patients as closed physiological entities, presuming that
deterioration following transfer stems entirely from built-in
disease progression. In clinical practice, however, ICU discharge is fundamentally a resource deployment and scheduling process carried out under institutional bed constraints
(Kim et al. 2015; Wagner et al. 2013; Long and Mathews
2018). Intensivists must routinely weigh incomplete physiological recovery against acute bed demands driven by emergency department (ED) boarding or post-operative transfers.
Patient transfer frequency before ICU admission, ED boarding durations, and ward acceleration driven by bed saturation
directly mirror operational workflow strain and care coordination friction; yet, customary doctrines remain entirely
agnostic to this systemic background. Second, models suffer

from data disparity and text template noise. Intensive care
records span high-frequency temporal streams, sparse diagnostic codes, and lengthy unstructured progress notes. While
recent studies explore medical text via knowledge graphs
(Lu, Nguyen, and Dou 2021) or multimodal architectures
for readmission prediction (Helmy et al. 2025; Datta et al.
2025), clinical free-text is often lengthy, laden with jargon,
and burdened by copy-pasted administrative templates. Directly feeding unadapted high-dimensional text embeddings
fails to yield meaningful gains and instead introduces severe
feature interference (negative transfer) with strong baselines
such as gradient-boosted decision trees.
To resolve these problems, we reframe ICU discharge
evaluation as an operationally aware multimodal decisionsupport task and develop a CDSS architecture that encompasses self-evolving textual distillation and deep cross-modal
correlation. The core contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
• Operation-aware Decision Framework: Overcoming randomized timestamp offsets in MIMIC-III, we introduce unit-benchmarked turnover deviation indicators
(icu_speedup_los) and care flow complexity features, eliminating the subjective assumption that outcomes depend solely on physiological status.
• Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Text Distillation: We formulate a 7-domain specialist agent collaborative division
of labor paired with a reflective evolutionary experience
repository, accumulating 3,085 rules across 5,000 patient samples. Combined with FAISS vector retrieval for
structured information distillation, we propose a layerwise, frozen, end-to-end fine-tuning scheme that systematically mitigates interference from template noise in clinical notes.
• Thorough Testing under Low-Prevalence Conditions:
Across a cohort of 20,000 ICU stays, we conduct 20 independent randomized multi-seed runs (Seeds 42–61),
exposing the weakness of single-modality physiological
prediction (PR-AUC of only 0.1387) and verifying the
robustness of the end-to-end fine-tuning architecture in
achieving global optimality (AUROC 0.8288, PR-AUC
0.4373, F1 0.4757).
• Interpretability & Clinical Utility Analysis: Through integrated gradients (Sundararajan, Taly, and Yan 2017),
we systematically explain the mechanistic contributions
of separate physiological channels, pivotal time-based attention windows, and operational flow variables in 30-day
readmission forecasting.
S2
Clinical Workflow and Problem
Formulation
S2.1
Clinical Prediction Task and Endpoint
Definition
The prediction target is unplanned ICU readmission within
30 days following discharge (30-Day Readmission). In light
of complex intra-hospital patient trajectories, endpoint labels
yi ∈{0, 1} follow strict clinical mutually exclusive criteria:
• Cross-admission readmission: The patient undergoes a
separate hospital admission and enters an ICU within
30 days post-discharge (same SUBJECT_ID, distinct
HADM_ID).
• Within-admission readmission: During the same hospitalization episode, the patient is transferred out of the
ICU, deteriorates, and is subsequently readmitted back to
the ICU (multiple ICU stays under the same HADM_ID,
where the subsequent admission timestamp postdates
the preceding transfer timestamp, i.e., INTIMEk+1 >
OUTTIMEk).
• Mortality Mutual-Exclusion: Patients who suffer inhospital mortality (HOSPITAL_EXPIRE_FLAG = 1)
have their readmission risk strictly calibrated to 0, eliminating logical contradictions.

S3
Operation-aware CDSS Architecture
S3.1
Unstructured Text Mining: Multi-Agent
Self-Evolving RAG Mechanism
Orchestrated multi-agent architectures sustain diagnostic fidelity under complex clinical workloads compared to monolithic models (Klang et al. 2026). To overcome verbosity
and repetitive administrative boilerplate in clinical progress
notes, the system establishes a self-evolving multi-agent distillation pipeline across five coordinated stages: (i) Hierarchical Specialist Collaboration: Clinical text is segmented
across 7 specialized clinical domains (Nursing, Radiology,
ICU Care, ECG, Attending Physician, Discharge Summary,
and Echocardiography), where independent domain agents
extract acute warning signals. (ii) Meta-Agent Synthesis: A
supervisory Meta-Agent aggregates domain summaries, synthesizing a structured JSON report containing a prioritized
vital factor roster and composite risk score. (iii) Reflective Evolution Loop: When predictions diverge from clinical ground truth during training, a Reflection Agent audits causal failure points, extracting physician-vetted heuristic rules into a knowledge base (3,085 rules); correctly
classified trajectories are cataloged as gold-standard exemplars (1,899 cases). (iv) Lightweight RAG Retrieval: Guided
by all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings and a FAISS index, the pipeline dynamically retrieves the Top-5 relevant heuristic rules and Top-1 exemplar case per admission as few-shot in-context demonstrations. (v) Feature Embedding & Projection: Distilled summaries are encoded
by Bio_ClinicalBERT into 768-dimensional contextual
vectors and projected to a d-dimensional embedding Enote
aligned with temporal representations.
S3.2
Cross-Modal Network Architecture and
Deep Fusion Schemes
The framework investigates three architectural paradigms.
First, we evaluate tree-based baselines: XGBoost (Chen and
Guestrin 2016) and LightGBM (Ke et al. 2017) fitted on
physically concatenated vectors combining temporal statistical aggregations, static covariates, multi-hot codes, and 768dimensional text embeddings (Chen et al. 2025). Second,
we construct sequential late fusion (LSTM Late Fusion):
a 4-layer bi-directional LSTM with self-attentive temporal
pooling encodes physiological sequences, concatenated at
the classification tier with static, code, and text representations projected via independent MLP branches. Third, we
formulate parallel multi-branch extraction and late fusion.
Dynamic physiological sequences are encoded via a deep
Transformer encoder (Vaswani et al. 2017) into Zcls; static
operational indicators and medical codes are mapped via independent MLPs into dense vectors Eops and Ecode. These
representations are concatenated with text embedding Enote
to form Zfused = [Zcls ∥Eops ∥Ecode ∥Enote] ∈R2d+64,
feeding a 2-layer MLP head for joint risk estimation. To prevent representational collapse during end-to-end training, the
initial 8 layers of Bio_ClinicalBERT are frozen, leaving deeper layers (Layers 9–12) active; BERT parameters
receive a 0.1× scaled learning rate (lr * 0.1) with gradient checkpointing enabled.
S3.3
Training Optimization and Leak-Free
Validation Protocols
To ensure leak-free evaluation under severe class imbalance (∼10.5% prevalence) and achieve stable multimodal convergence, we institute four disciplined methodological
safeguards:
(i)
Zero-Leakage
Normalization:
StandardScaler parameters are fitted strictly on training splits; validation and testing cohorts inherit these statistics without re-estimation, precluding distribution leakage.
(ii) Dynamic Cost-Sensitive Loss: To counter minority-class
sparsity, a weighted binary cross-entropy loss deploys dynamic inverse class ratio weighting derived strictly from
the training split: wpos = Nneg/Npos ≈8.97. (iii) Cosine
Scheduling & Rollback: A CosineAnnealingLR schedule regulates learning rate decay, paired with early stopping (patience=15) on validation loss; upon termination,
model parameters rollback to the checkpoint achieving the
minimal validation loss. (iv) Decision Threshold Calibration: On validation splits, exhaustive grid search (step size
0.01) determines the optimal decision cutoff t∗maximizing
the F1-Score, replacing the uncalibrated default 0.5 threshold
for final test evaluation.

S4.2
Comprehensive Performance Comparison
and Paradigm Defense
Table S1 summarizes the evaluation measures across 15
model configurations evaluated at their optimal F1 decision
thresholds across 20 randomized runs. Three core empirical insights emerge. First, we observe text negative transfer in shallow concatenation. Directly appending unadapted
768-d text embeddings into decision trees (XGBoost/LightGBM) triggers notable performance decline. Inpatient notes
are saturated with administrative templates and boilerplate
shorthand; high-dimensional embeddings diffuse greedy tree
node splitting, diluting compact, high-SNR physiological
and operational signals. Second, results demonstrate the
strength of structured baselines and multimodal reframing.
Purely structured LSTM Base establishes a solid benchmark (F1 = 0.4798), demonstrating the fitting efficiency of
recurrent cells over 48-hour dynamics along with discrete
codes. The multimodal goal is therefore not to surpass structured models trivially, but to resolve a harder clinical challenge: safely incorporating high-value text semantics without
suffering negative transfer (as observed in Transformer EarlyFusion, which degrades under raw text noise). Third, we
achieve a reversal of negative transfer via end-to-end finetuning. With layer-wise parameter freezing (Layers 9–12 active) and differential learning rates, Transformer E2E
FineTune is the sole architecture reversing text negative
transfer into positive gains, securing global peaks in both
AUROC (0.8288) and PR-AUC (0.4373). Paired hypothesis tests across 20 matched random seeds confirm that the
AUROC gain of end-to-end fine-tuning over static early fusion is highly statistically significant (mean AUROC 0.8288
vs. 0.8177; Paired t-test t = 4.89, p < 0.001; Wilcoxon
p < 0.001). This provides strong empirical evidence that
end-to-end backpropagation prevents the text representation
from being overshadowed by dense temporal dynamics, enabling the language model to better align textual risk cues
with structured physiological trajectories.

S4.4
Modal Ablation and Vitals Collapse
Feature ablation displays strong asymmetries across modalities. Relying exclusively on 48-hour vital signs (LSTM
Base (Only Vitals)) precipitates catastrophic collapse: AUROC plummets to 0.6232 and PR-AUC crashes
to 0.1387 (only marginally above the ∼10.5% prevalence
baseline), yielding an F1 of only 0.2213. This demonstrates the inherent insufficiency of unimodal physiological
surveillance: immediate vitals capture transient acute acuity,
whereas chronic comorbidities and systemic operational friction primarily govern 30-day outcomes. Conversely, static demographics and coding alone (Only Static & Codes)
sustain an AUROC of 0.8227 and PR-AUC of 0.4294
(|∆prob| = 0.1437), establishing patient baseline and operational pressures as basic priors, with dynamic vitals
(|∆prob| = 0.1014) providing acute trajectory corrections.
S5
Mechanistic Attribution and Case Study
S5.1
Multimodal Attribution: Channels,
Dynamics, and Operations
Integrated Gradients (IG) and attention analyses uncover collaborative mechanisms throughout modalities (Figure S2).
First, regarding vital sign dynamics, Heart Rate (32.0%) and
Temperature (22.2%) account for over half of physiological IG attribution, reflecting cardiovascular reserve compensation and occult infection as primary discharge stability determinants. Blood glucose (13.7%), SBP (12.5%), and
SpO2 (10.3%) supply secondary warnings against unmitigated glycemic swings and tissue hypoperfusion. Second,
with respect to time-based attention focus, attention concentrates heavily on initial admission (t ∈[0, 2] h, Hour 0
weight = 0.0806), showing that initial resuscitation difficulty
strongly anchors downstream readmission risk. Across the
48h trajectory, intermediate monitoring (12–36h, 42.3%)
and acute admission (0–12h, 37.9%) dominate reasoning,
while pre-discharge attention declines (36–48h, 19.7%), indicating that the model assesses stabilization effort rather
than superficial pre-discharge calm. Third, empirical findings establish the primacy of operational friction. Pre-ICU
transfer frequency (pre_icu_transfers) accounts for
28.2% of static ablation and 50.9% of continuous feature IG
attribution, dominating all covariates. Ward turnover pressure (icu_speedup_los) contributes 9.6% of ablation
and 20.1% of continuous IG, proving that bed-saturationdriven premature discharge elevates hidden deterioration
risks, alongside surgical count (surg_count) and gap.
S5.2
Typical Clinical Case Vignette
A 68-year-old patient admitted with respiratory failure
achieved stable vitals after 48 hours in the ICU (normal BP and HR). Conventional heuristics mirroring vitalsonly logic would categorize the patient as fully ready
for discharge. However, our CDSS detected that before
ICU admission, the patient spent over 16 hours boarding
in the emergency department and underwent two intrahospital transfers (pre_icu_transfers elevated); furthermore, the patient’s unit was under extreme bed strain
(icu_speedup_los notably positive). Synthesizing multimodal evidence, the CDSS triggered an elevated-risk alert
for handoff friction and metabolic instability, prompting the
intensivist to extend observation by 24 hours and recheck
blood gases, successfully avoiding premature discharge and
subsequent readmission.
S6
Conclusion
Synthesizing system development and 20 experimental runs,
we highlight four engineering lessons for actual clinical AI
adoption. First, we recognize the limits of vitals-only monitoring: the catastrophic failure of Only Vitals (F1 =
0.2213) proves that immediate telemetry reflects transient
acuity rather than medium-term prognosis, mandating holistic cross-modal modeling. Second, mitigating text template
noise is critical; because inpatient records are heavily contaminated with administrative boilerplate, layer-wise parameter freezing and differential learning rates are required to
avoid text negative transfer. Third, operational context acts
as an endogenous risk: pre-ICU transfer friction and turnover

S1.1
Study Population and Cohort Derivation
Protocol
This study derives its analytical cohort from the MIMICIII v1.4 (Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care III)
database (Johnson et al. 2016), which comprises comprehensive clinical records from all intensive care admissions
at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (N = 61, 532 ICU
stays across 46, 520 unique patients).
In contrast to conventional benchmark studies that apply
post-hoc demographic exclusions (e.g., arbitrarily dropping
pediatric/neonatal admissions or short stays), our study retains all real-world ICU care units (including MICU, CSRU,
CCU, SICU, TSICU, and NICU) to preserve clinical generalizability. The end-to-end cohort derivation protocol is
illustrated in Figure S3.
S1.2
Clinical Endpoint (Ground Truth) Definition
The primary target outcome is 30-Day Unplanned ICU
Readmission. In critical care environments, patients frequently transfer between emergency departments, surgical
suites, and step-down units. A positive binary ground truth
label (Y = 1) is rigorously assigned if either of the following
conditions holds:
1. Cross-Admission Readmission (Condition A): The
identical patient (indexed by ‘SUBJECT_ID‘) is discharged from the hospital and subsequently readmitted to
an ICU under a distinct hospital admission (‘HADM_ID‘),
where timestamps satisfy:
INTIMEnext > OUTTIMEcurrent
∧
INTIMEnext ≤OUTTIMEcurrent + 30 days
(S3)
2. Within-Admission
Bounce-Back
(Condition
B):
Within the same hospital encounter (‘HADM_ID‘), the
patient is transferred out of the ICU to a general or stepdown ward, acutely deteriorates, and is readmitted to an
ICU (INTIME > OUTTIME).
Competing Risk Censoring: In-hospital mortality represents a terminal competing risk that precludes future postdischarge readmission. Patients who expired during their hospital stay (HOSPITAL_EXPIRE_FLAG = 1) are censored
with Y = 0. In our final cohort of N = 20, 000 stays, exactly 2, 000 patients (10.00%) experienced 30-day readmission, yielding a natural class imbalance ratio of 1 : 9. During
training, this imbalance is calibrated via an inverse positive
loss weight wpos = Nneg/Npos ≈8.97.
