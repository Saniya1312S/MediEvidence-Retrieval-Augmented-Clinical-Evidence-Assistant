TWINGUARD-LITE: A Rule-Based State-Admission Gateway
for Generative Patient Digital Twins
Wenhui Chu and Sheikh Rabiul Islam
Department of Cybersecurity
College of Emergency Preparedness, Homeland Security and Cybersecurity
University at Albany, State University of New York
Albany, NY, USA
wchu3@albany.edu, sislam7@albany.edu
Abstract—Future generative patient digital twins may combine
longitudinal health records with language-model agents and
keep information across sessions. The wording of a proposed
update does not reveal whether it comes from an allowed
source, conflicts with the patient’s record, or belongs to
someone else. We present TWINGUARD-LITE, a rule-based
gateway that admits an update to a twin’s persistent state
only if it passes checkable approximations of two integrity
properties. Grounded state consistency (GSC) is approximated
by a provenance check and a contradiction check; cross-patient
noninterference (CPN) is approximated by a namespace check
against trusted transport metadata. We evaluate three attack
families over 20 seeded person-level splits of a semi-synthetic
stream built from the openly licensed eICU database demo,
with 29,131 ± 2706 candidate updates per seed. A keyword
filter and an anomaly detector detect 16% and 24% of attacks,
respectively; a provenance-only ablation detects 59.9% but
misses every cross-patient attack. Combining GSC and CPN
yields 99.1% precision, 90.5% recall, a 94.6% F1 score, and a
0.033% false-positive rate. Every attack the full gateway admits
falsely claims a trusted source; when the retrieval channel does
so, 92.9% of retrieval attacks are admitted. The results hold
under stated trust assumptions, and no language model, agent,
or retriever is executed: TWINGUARD-LITE is a mechanismlevel proof of concept that motivates authenticated, patientbound ingestion, not a clinical safeguard.
Index Terms—Agentic AI security, data provenance, patient
digital twins, prompt injection, state integrity
1. Introduction
Patient digital twins—patient-specific digital replicas
that inform clinical decisions—are still largely preclinical,
mostly simulation or monitoring models [1], [2]. We consider a future generative twin that keeps state across sessions
and acts through language-model agents and clinical tools.
Such a twin inherits the security problems of agentic AI:
indirect prompt injection [3], [4], retrieval poisoning [5],
memory poisoning [6], and poisoning of medical models [7].
Recent HealthSec work traces the same attack paths through
a healthcare system powered by large language models
(LLMs) [8]. The patient setting adds a distinctive asset:
a persistent, identity-bound clinical state. A corrupted fact
survives across sessions and, if patient namespaces are not
isolated, can spread between people.
Content filters and anomaly detectors are tempting first
defenses. The attacks that evade them, however, are textually
unremarkable: a fact phrased like a legitimate record but
lacking authorized provenance, a claim that silently contradicts an existing record, or a value copied from another
patient. Such a write is invalid because of its source, its
relation to the record, or its owner, not because of its wording. Following design-level prompt-injection defense [9] and
classical integrity policy [10], [11], we therefore enforce
patient-twin integrity through explicit rules checked on each
state update.
We make three contributions. First, we define four integrity properties, one for each of four relations a state
update has (Section 2). Second, we build TWINGUARDLITE, a rule-based gateway that enforces checkable approximations of two of them before any update persists
(Section 3). Third, we evaluate it on a semi-synthetic, realdata-backed benchmark with three attack families, two surface baselines, and a check-by-check ablation (Section 4).
Section 5 discusses limits.
2. Model and Integrity Properties
2.1. Twin State and Candidate Writes
A twin for patient namespace n at logical time t holds
a canonical state zn,t of accepted clinical facts, a persistent
memory, a capability set κn,t, and an append-only event and
provenance ledger. A patient is a person, not an encounter.
No input changes zn,t or κn,t directly: every input first
becomes a candidate write, a proposed update that carries
an event type, a claimed namespace, an origin label, an
episode (one hospital admission), an event time, and a value.
Inputs come from three trust zones (Fig. 1): authoritative
patient evidence (authenticated but possibly erroneous), curated knowledge (versioned reference material, including

untrusted context (free text, unvetted external content, model

2.2. Integrity Properties
We consider four relations a write stands in: to its source
and the record it joins, to its owner, to the sequence of states
before it, and to the actions it enables. Asking one question
about each relation defines one property.
Is it supported? Grounded state consistency (GSC):
every accepted fact f ∈zn,t has an unbroken provenance
path to authoritative evidence and violates no declared contradiction rule, unless an explicit unresolved-conflict state
records the violation.
Whose is it? Cross-patient noninterference (CPN): for
patients i ̸= j, changing only untrusted inputs in i’s history
cannot change the canonical state, memory, or action trace
of j, apart from a declared, minimized allow-list of shared
metadata that carries no patient-distinguishing signal.
Does it follow from the history? Longitudinal transition
integrity (LTI): the sequence of admitted states follows a
declared transition relation. What may it trigger? Authorized action integrity (AAI): every action taken on twin
state is permitted by an explicit action policy.
The four properties mirror classical integrity policy.
GSC corresponds to the Clark–Wilson rule that unconstrained inputs are either validated into constrained data
or rejected [10]. LTI corresponds to the Clark–Wilson requirement that transformations move data from one valid
state to another, and AAI to its rules on who may run
which transformation. CPN is noninterference [11] with
patients as the security domains and the allow-list as the
only sanctioned downgrading channel.
The properties do not collapse into one another: individually grounded writes can trace an illegal trajectory (LTI),
a grounded write can land in the wrong record (CPN),
admissible state can drive an unauthorized action (AAI),
and a correctly attributed write can be unsourced (GSC).
We do not claim completeness; we argue coverage against
Clark–Wilson instead. The append-only ledger realizes its
logging rule, while periodic verification of stored data and
separation of duty are not modeled. The set targets the
integrity of persistent state against an attacker who can propose writes. Legality of transitions over time falls under LTI;
interval-aware contradiction rules remain GSC checks on the
current record. Clinical correctness of authoritative evidence
is left to clinical validation, and confidentiality, availability,
and model integrity are out of scope. TWINGUARD-LITE
enforces per-write approximations of GSC and CPN, the
two properties that need no transition relation or action
policy. Because CPN relates two runs, the namespace check
enforces a stronger per-write condition: no write crosses
namespaces outside the allow-list. Under a trusted origin
binding, this approximates CPN for direct writes on the
canonical-state channel, the only channel implemented. Influence routed through shared stores, such as a retrieval
index, needs lineage tracking: the namespace check does
not see it, and only the GSC checks can stop it.
2.3. Threat Model and Trust Assumptions
The adversary seeks a target-state change, cross-patient
influence, or an unauthorized action, and enters through a
prompt, a retrieved document, a memory candidate, or a
tool output. We parameterize capability rather than assume
one universal attacker [12]. The evaluated attacker proposes
candidate writes, may spoof the mutable origin label, and
knows the frozen two-pair contradiction policy (Section 4.1),
but it is nonadaptive at run time. It cannot alter the policy
engine or the true originating namespace, which the benchmark supplies as trusted transport metadata.
Two assumptions bound every result. (i) CPN is evaluated given a correct binding between each write and the
patient it came from. A deployment must obtain that binding
from an authenticated session or a signed, patient-bound
event envelope. Our experiment validates the policy under
this binding but does not show how to establish the binding;
without the binding, CPN inherits the label-spoofing weakness of the provenance check (Section 3). (ii) Evaluation
starts once a poisoned item has become a candidate write.
We model neither index construction nor retrieval ranking,
so we study retrieval-mediated state poisoning rather than
end-to-end retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) poisoning.
3. TWINGUARD-LITE: A Rule-Based Gateway
Candidate writes from all three trust zones go through
the three checks of Fig. 1, described here from top to bottom.
(1) The provenance check (GSC) rejects a medication,
diagnosis, or laboratory candidate whose origin label is not
authoritative. It reads only the label: it builds no provenance
chain and verifies no signature, so it is cheap but spoofable.
Treatment candidates are outside its scope.
(2) The contradiction check (GSC) rejects a medication
candidate that forms a declared conflict pair with a medication already admitted to the same episode; drug names are
matched after normalization. The prototype implements no
unresolved-conflict state, so every match is rejected.
(3) The namespace check (CPN) rejects a candidate
whose true originating namespace, taken from trusted transport metadata, differs from the namespace it claims, unless
its type is on the frozen shared-metadata allow-list.
How the checks combine. Admission is a conjunction: a
write enters canonical state only if all three checks pass, and
any single failure rejects it. The checks are side-effect-free
predicates, so their order decides only which rule the ledger
records as the reason, never whether a write is admitted. We
chose conjunction because each check encodes a hard invariant, not a score; weighted or learned fusion would let strong
evidence on one property buy admission for a violation of
another. Beyond the ablation arms and surface baselines in

4. Preliminary Evaluation
4.1. Benchmark
Benign trajectories come from the eICU Collaborative
Research Database (eICU-CRD) Demo v2.0.1 [13], the
openly licensed subset of the full eICU-CRD [14]. Namespaces are people, not intensive care unit (ICU) stays: our
500 unit stays resolve to 362 people, and we split by
person, 253 for calibration and 109 (148 ± 7 stays) for
testing, so no person lies on both sides. Times in eICU are
offsets from each unit stay’s own admission. We therefore
rebuild a shared episode clock for each hospital admission
as offset −hospitalAdmitOffset and replay admitted medication state within each episode (424 episodes;
133 in the first seed’s test split). Events cover medications,
diagnoses, laboratory results, and treatments.
The cohort is the 500 smallest patientunitstayid
values among Demo stays with at least 20 extracted events.
This rule is exactly reproducible but not random: stay identifiers are not assigned at random with respect to hospital, and
the event threshold favors data-rich stays. The cohort spans
48 distinct de-identified values in the source hospitalid
field (dataset codes, not a verified count of real sites), so
the results should not be read as generalizing across sites
or acuities. Once missing payloads are removed, 3 stays fall
below 20 usable events (15 at the minimum); we keep them
rather than reselect. Drug names are missing for 6137 of the
16,335 medication rows (38%; 103,821 rows overall); they
are dropped at load, so no medication attack or vocabulary
uses a missing-value marker. However, 559 retained laboratory rows lack a numeric result, and cross-patient copies
of them are still rejected by the namespace check. The
medication table’s coded fields (drugHiclSeqno, GTC)
are not used.
Only the benign stream is observed data; every adversarial write, origin label, and namespace binding is generated by the benchmark. Three attack families are injected
into the test split, giving 29,131 ± 2706 candidates per
seed, 1090 ± 16 of them malicious. Direct/indirect injection has loud (instruction tokens), quiet (a plain fact), and
provenance-spoof (a forged authoritative label) subtypes.
Like the spoofs, retrieval-mediated state poisoning targets
the declared conflict pairs, but it carries a curated origin.
Cross-patient contamination copies an event type and its
exact value from a different person’s real stream. The spoof
attacker is targeted and causal: it reads only earlier records
in the episode it writes to and places a real surface form
of a declared-conflict drug at a strictly later episode time.
The contradiction rule matches each declared drug name as
a whole word anywhere in the payload, because eICU text
often opens with a package quantity (2 ML - DIGOXIN
0.25 MG/ML IJ SOLN). It covers only string-matchable
conflicts and, in this corpus, one of the two declared
pairs: apixaban never appears, so warfarin/apixaban never
fires and every contradiction decision comes from amiodarone/digoxin.
4.2. Defenses and Protocol
The surface baselines are a keyword filter over the
loud-injection generator’s own instruction tokens and an
anomaly detector. The detector scores each write by its percharacter negative log-likelihood under a Laplace-smoothed
fourth-order character Markov model fitted on calibration
text only, and flags scores at least two standard deviations
(2σ) above the mean score of benign calibration writes.
Two choices keep this baseline honest. Text is case-folded
and whitespace-collapsed before scoring, and every injected
medication value is a verbatim corpus string; otherwise
the detector would separate typography, not content. The
structured arms ablate the gateway check by check: provenance only, contradiction only, GSC only (provenance +
contradiction), CPN only (namespace), and TWINGUARDLITE (GSC+CPN). A no-defense arm flags nothing and
gives the attack-success baseline.

and per-family attack success (the fraction of malicious
writes admitted to canonical state). Each metric is computed per seed and macro-averaged, as mean ± population standard deviation (SD) over 20 seeds. This spread

and spoof-targeting rates are instead pooled over seeds,
as are the false-positive ratios in Section 4.3, a pooled

4.3. Results
Table 1 and Fig. 2 summarize the main run. The surface
baselines are weak for a structural reason. The keyword
filter recalls only 0.16: it catches every loud injection (1.00),
while quiet, spoofed, poisoned, and cross-patient writes
carry no token to match (0.00 on quiet injection, 0.00 on
the provenance spoofs). The anomaly detector recalls 0.24
(F1 = 0.190±0.017). It too sees mainly loud writes (0.84),
falling to 0.19, 0.17, 0.16, and 0.04 on quiet, spoofed,
retrieval-mediated, and cross-patient writes, respectively. It
also flags 0.049 ± 0.006 of the mean 28,041 benign writes
per seed, which rules it out as a standalone admission control. Provenance alone reaches recall 0.599 ± 0.009 with no
false positives but misses every cross-patient write, whose
origin is authoritative for its source patient. GSC alone
reaches 0.605 ± 0.009 and CPN alone 0.300 ± 0.005. Only
their combination reaches recall 0.905 ± 0.008 (precision
0.991 ± 0.006, F1 = 0.946 ± 0.005) at a 0.033% FPR, or
9.35 ± 5.75 rejected benign writes per seed, and it drives
retrieval-mediated poisoning and, given the trusted binding,
cross-patient contamination to 0% success.
Why the combined row is nearly a sum. The recall
and false-positive count of TWINGUARD-LITE are almost
exactly the sums of the GSC-only and CPN-only values.
This is not because one check is selected per write; every
write must pass all three. The two properties catch nearly
disjoint attacks, sharing only 1 of 19,742 pooled true positives (largely by construction; Section 5), and CPN adds no
false positives.
The contradiction check alone. By itself, the contradiction check reaches only recall 0.028 ± 0.008 (precision
0.764±0.141; 9.40±5.78 false positives per seed), because
it inspects only medication writes that name a declared drug.
Its pooled false positives rise from 187 (GSC only) to 188
because a malicious medication write that the provenance
check would have rejected is admitted and becomes a conflict partner of a later benign write. It is therefore not a
detector in its own right, but it is the only check that can stop
a write with a forged authoritative label. In the full gateway,
145 rejections are logged under it, all provenance spoofs
except 1 cross-patient write that the namespace check also
rejects. It thus stops 144 attacks that no other check stops but
causes all 187 benign rejections, more than it alone prevents;
its worth therefore depends on a better-sourced rule set.
That role matters because the 0% retrieval result reflects
trust-zone enforcement, not robustness to poisoned retrieval:
the provenance check rejects the family’s curated origin
first. We reran all seeds with the retrieval channel lying
about its origin (authoritative), so that only the contradiction check can still reject those writes. Then 92.9%
of that family is admitted, and TWINGUARD-LITE falls
to recall 0.626 ± 0.010 (F1 = 0.766 ± 0.008), with the
benign stream and its 187 false positives unchanged. The
residual 24% injection success in the main run likewise
consists entirely of provenance spoofs. Only 171 of 2211
spoofs (7.7%) could be targeted at all—the rest landed in
episodes with no drug whose declared partner the attacker
could supply—and the check caught 84% of those, or 6.5%
of all spoofs. Contradiction checking is a partial backstop
against spoofing, not a substitute for authenticated ingestion.
With the strictest episode boundary, where no state crosses
a unit stay (500 episodes), the conclusions hold: recall
0.904 ± 0.008, precision 0.993 ± 0.004, injection residual
24%, 7.25 ± 4.25 false positives per seed, and 134 pooled
contradiction true positives.

whose episodes contain both drugs (9.35 per seed; repeated
splits retest the same records, so these are decisions, not

5. Discussion and Limitations
We discuss the main limits, including those raised in
review, and our view of each.
Strength by construction. The benign data is real, but the
origin labels, namespace bindings, and attacks are generated,
so the structured checks are strong partly by construction.
Likewise, the keyword list is the loud generator’s own, so
its 1.00 loud recall is guaranteed, and neither baseline is
state of the art. The near-disjointness of GSC and CPN
detections also follows from the family construction, and the
injection and retrieval families violate the same provenance
check, so their separate rates report attack provenance, not
independent defenses.
Trusted patient binding. CPN’s 0% cross-patient success
holds given a trusted true-origin binding. Once that binding
exists, detecting a crossing is simple; the hard problem is
establishing the binding through authenticated sessions or
signed, patient-bound event envelopes, and this work does
not solve it. The CPN allow-list is also unvalidated and
unexercised: no evaluated write uses an allow-listed type, so
the CPN numbers are those of a deny-every-crossing rule.
Authenticated provenance. The provenance check reads a
label, and the spoofed-retrieval run shows the consequence:
92.9% of lying retrieval writes are admitted. Authenticated,
origin-bound provenance is therefore central to making the
approach usable, and the label check stands in for it here.
The check also covers only medication, diagnosis, and laboratory writes, so the 12,172 treatment rows (12% of the
corpus) pass it unexamined. No attack exploits this gap:
treatment-type attacks occur only as cross-patient copies,
which the namespace check rejects.
Contradiction coverage. The declared list holds two drug
pairs that we chose as demonstration policy, not drawn from
an interaction authority, guideline, or prespecified protocol. It therefore carries a cherry-picking risk as well as a
coverage limit, and the reported contradiction recall is an
upper bound conditional on the list. Our extraction omits
drugStopOffset, so medication state is cumulative and
a flagged pair may never have been active at the same
time. Even an active pair need not be an error: amiodarone
with digoxin, the pair behind every false positive here, can
be prescribed deliberately with a reduced digoxin dose. A
larger, clinically grounded rule set with medication start and
stop times would make the evaluation more convincing and
reduce such rejections. Until then, flagged writes should be
quarantined for clinician adjudication, with the ledger entry
and rule attached, rather than silently rejected.
End-to-end evaluation. No language model, agent, or
retriever runs here. Starting at the admission boundary
isolates the mechanism: the decision depends only on the
candidate write, its transport metadata, and the admitted
state, though the producing pipeline sets the fields the checks
read, notably the origin label. It does not show how often,
or in what form, real injected or retrieved content becomes a
candidate write. A small end-to-end study, in which an LLM
agent with retrieval produces writes that reach the gateway,
is the most important next experiment. The attacker is also
nonadaptive at run time. Ongoing work adds authenticated
ingestion, a maintained rule set with medication intervals,
LTI and AAI enforcement, adaptive attackers, and patientclustered statistics.
6. Related Work
Agentic prompt-injection benchmarks and defenses [3],
[4], [9], [15], retrieval and memory poisoning [5], [6], and
medical-model poisoning [7] establish the individual attack
surfaces, and adversarial machine learning taxonomies [12]
structure our threat model. At HealthSec’25, Nagaraja and
Bahsi [8] apply goal-driven risk assessment to an LLMpowered healthcare system, including prompt injection and
knowledge-base poisoning, and identify cross-patient context contamination through poor memory isolation as a risk;

--- PAGE 6 [pdf_text] ---
CPN addresses the integrity side of that risk for persistent state. At HealthSec’24, Hallajiyan et al. [16] anticipate inference-time false data injection attacks on machinelearning-enabled medical devices. Both study how corrupted
inputs reach clinical systems; we add an enforcement point
for the state they leave behind.
CaMeL [9] stops untrusted data from steering control flow within one execution; TWINGUARD-LITE governs
what an execution may persist across executions. Neither
subsumes the other: a protected run can still commit an
unsourced fact, and CPN quantifies over a second patient’s
history, which no single trace expresses.
7. Conclusion
Attacks that evade surface filters are textually ordinary
but structurally invalid. Enforcing rule-level approximations
of GSC and CPN as auditable admission checks detects
90.5% of the malicious writes here at a 0.033% falsepositive rate. Every admitted attack is a provenance spoof,
calling for authenticated ingestion, and every rejected benign
write comes from the contradiction rule, calling for rules
from a maintained interaction authority. The main practical
takeaway is a workflow for systems that persist patient state:
bind every write to an authenticated source and patient,
check it against declared rules before it persists, log each
decision, and quarantine what the rules flag. As no language
model, agent, or retriever is run, these results validate defined properties under stated trust assumptions, not clinical
readiness.
Ethics and Data
Benign streams derive from the open-access eICU-CRD
Demo v2.0.1 [13], which PhysioNet [17] distributes under
the Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0 without a credentialing agreement; it is a de-identified subset of the full
eICU-CRD [14]. Privacert certified its re-identification risk
as meeting the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Safe Harbor standard (Certification
no. 1031219-2). The study involves no interaction with
human subjects and no direct identifiers, and we make no
attempt to re-identify anyone; our derived tables remain
under ODbL 1.0 with attribution. All adversarial writes
are benchmark-generated; no real eICU record is labeled
malicious.
